The deadline is already closer than the migration

NCSC guidance expects cryptographic discovery complete by 2028, priority upgrades by 2031 and full migration by 2035. Discovery is the step that cannot be skipped, and it is the step almost nobody has done — because cryptography is the one dependency enterprises have never inventoried.

It is scattered

Keys and algorithms live in application code, infrastructure configuration, third-party libraries, hardware modules and firmware that outlives its support window.

It is invisible

No system holds the register. Ask any engineering team which algorithms they depend on and the honest answer is that they would have to go and look.

It is already exposed

Anything with a long confidentiality lifetime is exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later today — the traffic is being collected before the quantum computer exists.

Three steps from unknown to sequenced

1

Discover

Static analysis across Python, JavaScript, Java, Go and server configuration, X.509 certificate inspection, and runtime observation of what your systems actually negotiate on live connections — including cryptography inside vendor binaries whose source you will never see.

2

Classify

Every finding is graded by quantum exposure — broken outright by Shor's algorithm, weakened by Grover's, or holding adequate margin — and separately by whether it is unsafe today. RSA, ECC and DH are quantum-vulnerable whatever their key size. MD5, SHA-1 and 3DES are already broken.

3

Sequence

You get a CycloneDX CBOM an auditor can read, a prioritised migration queue ordered by exposure and effort, and the named replacement for each asset — ML-KEM for key establishment, ML-DSA for signatures.

Questions

Do you see our source code?

No. The tool runs inside your own environment and makes no outbound calls. Nothing leaves your perimeter. On the assessment tier an engineer reviews findings with your team, but the code itself is never transferred to us.

How is this different from a free CBOM generator?

Free tools produce an inventory. The inventory is not the hard part — deciding what to migrate first, proving to your auditor that the work is sequenced, and catching cryptography that only exists inside compiled dependencies all are. You are buying the register and the roadmap, and the scanner that produces them accurately.

We do not use post-quantum algorithms anywhere. Is there anything to find?

Almost certainly yes, and that is the point. Every RSA, ECDSA, ECDH and Ed25519 usage in your estate is quantum-vulnerable regardless of key size, and long-lived confidential data is already exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later. Most estates also hold MD5, SHA-1 or 3DES somewhere, which are unsafe today regardless of quantum.

What do we actually receive?

A CycloneDX 1.6 CBOM in a standard format your tooling can consume, a migration report with a prioritised queue, and on the assessment tier a roadmap with sequencing and effort plus an executive summary written for a non-technical reader.

Can it run in CI?

Yes. The CLI takes a path, writes the CBOM and report, and returns a non-zero exit code when quantum-vulnerable cryptography is found — so it can gate a pipeline exactly as a linter would.
